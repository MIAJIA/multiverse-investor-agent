Problems the judge raised:

- C1: The three plans share one cond.cpo_volume dependency and carry no numeric threshold. The branches are three hypothetical terminals that all rely on the same chain, not competing branches. The Tower $1B run-rate in 4Q26 could have served as a grounded numeric condition, but it is only a terminal.
- C1: Several entry conditions are already met at the anchor date (cond.nvda_commit, cond.ext_laser_obs, cond.cpo_production), so they gate nothing. The LITE and COHR plans do not require the volume event as an entry condition even though it is their key dependency. The key_dependency 'why' for LITE also talks about SiPho wafer demand, which is a copy-paste mismatch.
- C1: Edge evidence is thin: h.start→cpo_volume and the SiPho wafer links are model-only, and Tower's growth is not tied to CPO specifically. sc-033 and the Broadcom volume-manufacturing line in sc-035 go unused in the nodes. Most nodes are labelled 'unknown' even though they are cited.
- C2: Offers no conditional WAIT plan or event conditions (formal filing, effective date, licence requirement), which the case asks for. A scenario-style plan gated on the user defining 'Fable' could have been provided.
- C2: Some excerpt stances are confusing: sc-044 'contradicts' and sc-050/051 'contradicts' do not map cleanly onto a no_chain verdict, and sc-046 (a GAO CRA decision) is included without explanation.
- C2: The abstention is arguably too strong. The pack shows chip-licensing effects on NVDA (a $5.5B H20 charge), so a plausible branch (model restriction leading to chip restriction) could have been sketched as low-confidence.
- C4: It never explicitly addresses the LITE excerpts (sc-039/sc-040) or says why they do not support an election-to-LITE chain; its reasoning centers on chip makers and export controls rather than the optical target.
- C4: The hop chain is framed around BIS and China revenue, which is only loosely relevant to an optical-infrastructure name like LITE, so the reasoning is somewhat tangential to the stated target.
- C4: The GAO/CRA mention is slightly speculative and the output says nothing about the proposals list, though 0 candidates implies it is empty.
- D1: The transits threshold_note does not cite the pre-closure baseline hz-009 (about 85-92 per day). It offers only the 3.7 closed-state and 24 post-MoU reference points. The reviewer specifically wanted hz-009 cited and the user asked to confirm what level counts as recovery. hz-009 appears in the excerpts but is not used.
- D1: Several edges label evidence as 'contradicts' and then rebut it as actually supportive, such as hz-011 and hz-013 on the reopen-to-transits edge. This is confusing stance labeling. The transits-to-flows edge is model_only with no evidence.
- D1: The 'materially above 3.7 per day' rule is vague, so the transits condition is hard to decide. The brent_rebound exit has no level. The reopen condition 'declared open' is also weak given that an announcement is not real traffic recovery.
- B1: The expected answer was no_chain, or at least no evidenced branch. The agent built a full chain to MU anyway, and its CPO -> memory link goes through generic AI-demand hops, which dilutes the fact that the thesis has no evidence.
- B1: The edge cluster_scale -> ai_demand is marked model_only=false, but it cites generic AI-demand excerpts (sc-007, sc-014) that say nothing about CPO enabling larger clusters. The 'supported' status is overstated.
- B1: Entry conditions only track the CPO ramp and cluster scale, not memory demand or MU results. They could be met without the thesis being right, so the plan does not test the memory link. The 804-day horizon is also arbitrary.
- B2: The case asks for conditions that a specific public data series can decide. Every condition has series null, comparator null and threshold null, so none is numerically decidable. Revenue growth is 'the threshold the user has set', which is not a rule.
- B2: The demand-observed condition is nearly trivially met. The CEO quote in sc-039, dated 2026-08-11, already says demand is increasing, and the rule accepts any filing or release stating 'increasing demand'. Removing it would leave the thesis unchanged, and it does not test whether adoption is faster than expected.
- B2: The thesis is faster-than-expected adoption, but no condition is benchmarked against expectations (consensus, guidance or TrendForce forecasts). Several rebuttals are strained: sc-038 and sc-037 put broad CPO inflection after 2027, which cuts against the thesis, yet they are dismissed. The shock node also has an unknown status while the plans depend on it.
- B3: The long plan has only one exit condition (sustained reopening). The independent risks the case expects are missing: bypass pipeline recovery (hz-003/hz-022) and risk premium fade (hz-023). ANY over a single condition is meaningless.
- B3: The pack's own excerpts hz-003 and hz-022 are treated only as rebutted contradictions, and hz-023 is not used at all. These should be separate observable exit triggers, so the plan lacks a real OR structure.
- B3: The short plan's exit conditions are the long plan's entry conditions, a mirror that adds padding rather than independent risks. Brent elevation has no series, source or threshold, so it is not observable. The reopening exit has no numeric level or window start, only a scenario anchor, so it is not clearly dated.
- B4: The rival long branch carries the user's original short statement as its user_judgment, and its entry condition anchors to an EIA STEO date (2026-10-19) rather than the reopening scenario. This is incoherent with the thesis and invents a document anchor.
- B4: The short's entry requires transits >=92/day, a full pre-closure recovery. This is far stricter than the user's stated trigger, and may never be met within 72 hours, so it quietly changes the thesis. The 2026-12-31 deadline is also an assumed absolute date.
- B4: The 72-hour window is only in the rule text, not structured, and the no-attack node is also marked shock, though it is a non-event. Some cited support is thin, such as hz-019 reused for the premium edge.
- B6: The sc-025 rebuttal leans on an unsupported claim that the same excerpt describes higher-power ELS content per unit; the excerpt says nothing about Lumentum content or share, so the 4x reduction is under-weighted and not tied to any revenue sizing.
- B6: The entry conditions are weak and loosely tied to the thesis: 'observed_by' conditions with a deadline of 2027-12-31 and no thresholds, so the plan cannot say when to enter. The CPO shipment condition is already largely met per sc-035, and the exit condition (no lift reported by the end of 2027) is very slow.
- B6: The e.nvda_deal and e.cpo_ramp nodes are marked evidence_status 'unknown' despite direct T1 support, while the intermediate and terminal nodes are marked 'supported' on the same evidence. The sc-014 support is NVIDIA generic 10-K boilerplate, not specific to Lumentum.
- R2: The exit has no observable event trigger such as a renewed closure, attack or IRGC declaration. The 'transits stall' exit only fires at the 9/30 deadline, so it would not have fired on 7/12. The Brent exit is also just an end-of-horizon check.
- R2: The agent sets a 85.5 threshold itself instead of leaving it to the user (null plus note). hz-015 (Maersk) is cited in the excerpts but not used in any edge or the key dependency. The hz-010 rebuttal also somewhat downplays the precedent.
- R2: The hz-009 and hz-010 excerpts are dated 2026-10-14, after the 2026-06-21 backtest cutoff, which suggests possible leakage of later data. The 'a.priced' assumption and the premium edge have no evidence.
- R3: The key dependency is set to licensed shipments starting, not revenue actually recognised and disclosed. Revenue confirmation is the true dependency. A single small licensed shipment would meet the entry condition and still leave revenue far from recovering, as happened here.
- R3: The rebuttals are too dismissive. sc-030 and sc-031 are marked as contradicting but are waved off as 'direction holds', and the sc-032 rebuttal reads the scope of D:5 and Macau on thin grounds. The 50% cap and third-party testing may bind volume, yet the plan sets no volume or revenue threshold. The `h.revenue` node has no condition tied to it.
- R3: Weak structure: the h.shipments node is labelled 'supported' though its only support is the rule itself, and the e.rule node is 'unknown' despite a T1 source. The substitution assumption has no evidence, and tariff or other cost frictions are not considered.
- K3: The entry condition is weak. It only needs output in November or December to be below October's, which a 50% cut or even a minimal one would satisfy. It sets no minimum size and does not name the early-December survey as the deciding data.
- K3: The rebuttals of contradicting evidence lean on 'partial binding' and do not engage the key point from op-001/op-002/op-004. The shortfall sits mostly with Russia and non-OPEC members, so the real cut may be much smaller. The graph also keeps the headline-to-price edge as 'supported' on a price jump alone.
- K3: The price entry condition (Dec22 settles above the 10/5 settle on any session) is already nearly met given the +$8 move. The 'any session' rule is trivial, so the plan could trigger a long without real confirmation. The no-physical exit and the 87-day horizon are loosely specified.
- T1: The 'plausibly exogenous' wording is slightly hedged, and it does not say outright that treatment assignment by a fixed exposure threshold was not driven by expected volatility outcomes.
- T1: It does not name specific confounders to check, such as concurrent capital-flow or macro events, or the possibility that managers shifted holdings around the 50% threshold.
- T2: Misses the reference logic: the liquidity crunch depressed most asset classes, so the inflation-indexed bond shift is the plausible necessary trigger for the return increase. The answer gives the wrong label (AMBIGUOUS instead of YES).
- T2: Treats the liquidity crunch as a rival explanation for Y, when the material says it affected most assets and so points to returns falling. That reverses the evidence's implication.
- T2: Demands a rigorous counterfactual the task never expects, which makes the answer over-cautious. It does not weigh the scenario's structural framing, in which the hedge was deliberately made against inflation.
- T3: Does not state the backdoor structure clearly: Z affects both the decision to buy and yields. It frames Z mostly as a rival cause, and the claim that Z drove the purchase decision is not tied to the evidence.
- T3: Bundles 'inflation expectations' and 'stimulus' into one common cause. The material only says inflation expectations were rising when the analyst bought, so this blurs the two.
- T3: Does not point out that the stimulus lowered bond prices, which mechanically raises yields. It also does not say what would resolve the ambiguity, such as controlling for Z.
- T4: Treats 'predicts' and 'causes' as the same thing. Predictive value can exist under confounding or reverse causation, so the answer should separate 'not causal' from 'not predictive', and the claim that it is 'not supported' is a little loose.
- T4: Does not say what evidence would change the verdict, such as lead-lag timing tests or controlling for instability.
- T4: Says the material 'explicitly attributes' the association to a common cause, which slightly overstates the source, though it does match the text.
- T5: Does not explicitly flag that the timing of the macro improvement relative to the announcement is unknown, which the reference treats as crucial.
- T5: Does not note that the peer rise could be partly a sector spillover from the announcement, or that the dividend could still have a smaller effect; the final line is a bit generic.
- T6: Does not state the key conditional from the reference: if the market expected continued hikes, prices would likely fall (claim fails); if it expected stabilization or cuts, they might not (claim holds). It gives no explicit conditional answer.
- T6: Leans on confounding by inflation expectations, which is a side issue. It underuses the 'unexpected' hike and the conflicting sentiment (Z) as the main source of ambiguity about how the market read the hike.
