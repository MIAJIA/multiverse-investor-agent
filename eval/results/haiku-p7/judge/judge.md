Problems the judge raised:

- C1: The counter-evidence is partly explained away. sc-036, sc-037 and sc-038 are marked contradicts, yet each rebuttal says it is not a real rebuttal, and the claim is still kept as supported. sc-043 (integrated-laser route) and sc-025 appear only in the excerpts and rationales, not on any edge, so the required handling of them is incomplete.
- C1: Node and evidence statuses look inconsistent. e.sipho_680m is marked unknown even though sc-041 reports it directly, while i.cpo_volume_2026 is marked supported despite three contradicting excerpts. The key causal link from CPO volume to Tower SiPho demand is unsupported, and Tower's SiPho business is not shown to be tied to CPO. The thesis therefore rests on one company's revenue.
- C1: The branching is thin: a single hypothetical branch and one plan, with the Lumentum candidate never inspected. The entry condition on the $680M run rate has already been met (published 2026-08-04), so it adds little. The exit condition on missing $1B may not be checkable by the deadline, since Q4 results are likely published after 2026-12-31.
- C2: The pack's excerpts (AI Diffusion rule with model-weight controls, its rescission, the pulled chip draft, H20/H200 licences) are not used in the chain. The AI Diffusion rule's ECCN 4E091 model-weight control is directly relevant precedent, but the output lists it only as 'contradicts' and never reasons about it. The path therefore looks ungrounded despite relevant evidence.
- C2: The causal logic is questionable. Restricting overseas access to models is assumed to reduce NVDA accelerator demand, but model export limits could shift demand or even raise domestic compute demand. This counter-argument is not discussed, and the edge signs are all '+' on a thesis of falling demand.
- C2: The 180-day short horizon and the 2027-04-19 deadline are not justified. The second entry condition, 'overseas customers losing access', is hard to decide with official data, and the plan says the thesis is unsupported yet still lays out a short.
- C4: It does not mention LITE or the LITE excerpts (sc-039/sc-040) directly, so a reviewer cannot confirm they were considered and rejected.
- C4: The request to confirm the definition of a split Congress is a minor tangent, since the main blocker is the missing causal link.
- C4: The answer cites a broad range (sc-029 to sc-049) instead of specific excerpts, which makes the claims harder to verify.
- D1: Some rebuttals are a little generous. For example, hz-011 and hz-012 are framed as magnitude or persistence risks rather than as weakening the thesis that reopening restores transits. That wording understates how much the June precedent undercuts the user's view.
- D1: The key dependency is inventory restocking, yet the pack has no inventory series. Entry for the short plan hinges on conditions with null series and thresholds, so it is not fully decidable. The sticky-long entry also requires an inventory build, which is not a clean signal.
- D1: The abstention-like caution is mild. The 13-day horizon and the short side are specified even though the pack gives no reopening date. The rationale does say 'not recommended' for acting now, so the effect is limited.
- B1: Should have returned no_chain: the CPO-to-memory-demand link has no evidence, yet it builds a full chain and a hypothetical MU long branch, with the thesis resting on model-only hops.
- B1: The a.mu_supplier edge is not marked model_only and attaches sc-013 as evidence. Its mechanism says 'accelerator demand can reach Micron', which turns a supplier list into a demand-transmission claim. The key dependency and entry condition also rest on this supplier-list fact, which says nothing about CPO.
- B1: Intermediate nodes such as h.ai_scaleout and h.accel_volume are marked 'supported' using generic evidence (TPU v4 paper, CoWoS capacity), which inflates the apparent strength of a chain whose key hops are unevidenced. The note also references 'm.5 to m.8', an inconsistent node ID.
- B2: Fails the case's core test: every entry and exit condition has series null, comparator null and threshold null. Conditions such as 'a filing states revenue or orders increased' are close to vacuous and cannot show adoption is faster than expected. A numeric series was available, e.g. Lumentum's reported quarterly revenue or segment revenue from 10-Q/8-K.
- B2: Exit conditions rely on vague sources ('TrendForce or company reports', 'Industry research reports') and on subjective rules ('credible report states delayed'). They have no schedule and no way to be decided. The pluggable-dominance exit also nearly holds already per sc-038, which makes it a weak trigger.
- B2: The conditions test only whether Lumentum's CPO revenue is growing, not whether adoption is faster than expected, so the shock node is never tested. Removing the orders condition would barely change the thesis. Several nodes are marked 'unknown' evidence status despite having cited support.
- B3: Only one exit condition (reopening deal) is given, though exit_logic is ANY. The brief expects several independent exits: real transit recovery against the hz-009 baseline, bypass pipeline restoration (hz-003, hz-022), and fading of the risk premium (hz-023). The bypass and risk-premium risks appear in the graph and the key dependency but never become observable exit conditions.
- B3: The reopening exit mixes a Reuters announcement with a sustained rise in PortWatch transits above a user-confirmed threshold. That blends an announcement with the real event and depends on a threshold that has not been set. It has no Brent price exit or stop condition and no dated checkpoint before the deadline.

- B6: The sc-025 rebuttal rests on an unevidenced assumption that content per laser rises. The reduction is a factor of four versus legacy designs, and the claim that it merely caps growth is asserted rather than shown.

- R2: Most causal edges are model-only with no evidence. The hz-003 bypass edge's rebuttal is an inference, and the hz-009 excerpt is dated 2026-10-14, after the backtest date, which is a possible leak.
- R3: The frictions are dismissed in rebuttals rather than carried into the plan. The 50% cap, third-party testing and D:5/Macau denial do not appear in the entry conditions or the terminal. The shipment node is only 'greater than zero', which is too weak for 'revenue recovers'.

Marking h.h200_licenses as 'supported' is also too strong, since only the rule change is evidenced. Possible Chinese-side obstacles and the tariff are not considered.

The output dismisses this as only about event probability and does not lower the plan's weight or flag it prominently. The FOMC dates (2026-10-27/28) and the 3.7% CPI figure are not backed by any shown excerpt, and the date field of the excerpts is 'unknown'.
- W1: The term-premium exit condition for the long plan and the entry condition for the short plan are not truly independent of the cut. The long plan is also mostly supported by one excerpt on stable inflation expectations, which only weakly supports the mechanism. The mechanism evidence is thin overall, and the 'contradicts' stances are mostly irrelevant to the edge they are attached to.
